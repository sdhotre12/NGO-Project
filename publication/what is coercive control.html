Coercive Control: Understanding, Recognizing, and Ending Hidden Abuse

1. What is Coercive Control?

Coercive control is a form of psychological abuse where an abuser manipulates, isolates, and dominates someone to gain power. Unlike physical abuse, it often leaves no visible marks, making it harder to recognize.

  • Limits your independence or freedom.
  • Manipulates thoughts, feelings, and actions.
  • Creates fear, anxiety, or dependency.
  • Examples: controlling finances, monitoring activities, threats, gaslighting.

2. Signs You Might Be Experiencing Coercive Control

  • Feeling isolated from friends and family.
  • Constant anxiety, fear, or confusion in the relationship.
  • Feeling like you “cannot do anything right” or always apologizing.
  • Loss of confidence, independence, or ability to make decisions.
  • Restrictions on where you go, who you meet, or what you do.

3. How to Recognize Coercive Control

  • Pay attention to patterns rather than isolated incidents.
  • Notice if someone consistently manipulates or intimidates you.
  • Keep a record of controlling behaviors, threats, or restrictions.
  • Trust your instincts – if something feels wrong, it probably is.

4. How to Protect Yourself and Take Control

  • Set clear boundaries and communicate them safely.
  • Reach out to trusted friends, family, or professionals for support.
  • Document incidents of controlling behavior for evidence.
  • Engage in self-care and empowerment activities to strengthen your confidence.

5. Ending Coercive Control

  • Understand that abuse is never your fault.
  • Seek support from NGOs, helplines, or legal authorities.
  • Create a safety plan to gradually distance yourself from the abuser.
  • Consider counseling or therapy to recover emotionally and mentally.

Remember: Coercive control is hidden abuse, but it is real and damaging. Recognizing the signs and seeking help are the first steps toward reclaiming your freedom and well-being.
